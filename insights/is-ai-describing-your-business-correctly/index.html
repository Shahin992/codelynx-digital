Is AI Describing Your Business Correctly? A Practical Checklist for 2027

Share

Picture a homeowner who needs a new garage door opener installed. Before calling anyone, she opens an AI assistant on her phone and asks which companies in her area do the work and whether any offer weekend appointments. In a few seconds she has a tidy, confident answer. It names two of your competitors, and it says your company only handles commercial doors, which has never been true. She never visits your website. You never find out she existed.

This kind of quiet mistake is becoming more common as more people turn to AI tools for quick research. Assistants summarize what they can find about a business across the web. When that information is thin, outdated or contradictory, the summary can be wrong in ways that cost you real customers, and you may never see it happen.

The encouraging part is that you have more influence over these answers than most business owners assume. You cannot edit an AI model directly, but you can shape the information it learns from. The clearer and more consistent your facts are across the web, the more likely an assistant is to describe you accurately.

This checklist walks you through the process step by step. You will learn how to run a simple audit of what AI assistants currently say about your business, how to correct problems where they start, how to make your website easier for machines to understand and how to build a routine that keeps the picture accurate heading into 2027 and beyond.

Why AI answers about your business matter now

For most of the internet's history, a search led to a list of links. A potential customer would scan several results, click through to a few websites and form their own opinion. AI assistants change that sequence. They do the reading on the customer's behalf and hand back a short summary, often naming just a few businesses.

That summary carries a lot of weight. People tend to trust a well-written answer, especially when it arrives instantly. If the assistant says you close at 5 p.m., many people will not double-check. If it leaves you out of a list of recommended providers, they may never know you were an option. The first impression of your business is increasingly formed somewhere you do not directly control.

It helps to understand where these answers come from. Some assistants draw mainly on what they learned during training, which can be months or even years old. Others search the web in real time and pull from whatever pages they find for the question. Many do a mix of both. In every case, the raw material is public information about you: your website, your business profiles, directory listings, review sites, news mentions and social media pages.

That is why this matters for every type of business, not just technology companies. A dental clinic, a law firm, a manufacturer and a neighborhood bakery are all being described by AI tools right now. The real question is whether those descriptions are accurate, and whether you are doing anything to shape them.

Step 1: Ask the assistants what they know

Start with a hands-on audit. Pick two or three of the AI assistants your customers are most likely to use and ask each of them the same set of questions. Use a private or logged-out browser window if you can, so the answers are not shaped by your own past conversations. Phrase your questions the way a real customer would, not the way a marketer would.

  • "What does [your business name] do, and where are they located?"
  • "What are the hours and phone number for [your business name]?"
  • "Who are the best [your service] providers in [your city or area]?"
  • "Is [your business name] a good choice for [a specific job or need]?"
  • "How does [your business name] compare with [a main competitor]?"

Record every answer in a simple spreadsheet, including the date, the assistant you used and the exact wording it gave. Mark each fact as correct, incorrect or missing. Pay close attention to your services, location, service area, hours, contact details and any claims about pricing or reputation. Note whether you appear at all in the recommendation questions, and how you are described next to competitors.

Look for patterns rather than one-off oddities. If several assistants repeat the same wrong phone number, it is almost certainly coming from a real source somewhere on the web. If one assistant is confused about which services you offer, check whether your own website explains them clearly. Each mistake is a clue that points back toward information that needs fixing.

Step 2: Fix the facts at the source

AI assistants can only be as accurate as the information they find. If your website lists one set of hours, your business profile lists another and an old directory still shows the address you moved away from, the assistants have to guess. Your job is to remove the guesswork by making every source agree.

Begin with the core facts: your exact business name, address, phone number, website address, opening hours, service area and main categories of work. Decide on one official version of each and write it down. Then check every place your business appears online. That includes your profiles on the major map and search platforms, industry directories, local business associations, social media pages, review sites and any old listings created by former staff or previous marketing vendors.

Consistency is the foundation. A single outdated listing can quietly undo a lot of good work.

Update or claim each listing so it matches your official version. Where you find duplicate listings, follow the platform's process to merge or remove them. Do not forget the less obvious places, such as supplier "where to buy" pages, partner websites and old press releases that mention a previous location. A manufacturer that relocated or a law firm that changed its name can have years of outdated mentions scattered around the web. Step 3: Make your website easy for machines to read

Your website should be the most authoritative source of information about your business. To play that role, it has to be easy for both people and machines to understand. Good design helps a visitor find what they need in seconds. Clear structure does the same job for the automated systems that read your pages.

Start with the basics. Each main service should have its own page with a descriptive title and a plain-language explanation of what you do, who it is for and where you offer it. Avoid vague taglines like "solutions for tomorrow" where a simple statement such as "commercial roof repair across the northern suburbs" would be more useful. Your About page should say clearly who you are, how long you have been in business and what makes you different. Your contact page should list every location with full address details.

A dedicated FAQ page is one of the most valuable additions you can make. Gather the questions your team hears every week by phone and email, then answer them directly and honestly. Questions about price ranges, turnaround times, insurance, warranties and service areas are exactly what customers ask AI assistants, so give those assistants a clear answer they can repeat.

Add structured data

Structured data, sometimes called schema markup, is a standardized way of labeling information on your pages so machines can read it without guessing. It can tell search engines that a string of numbers is your phone number, that a block of text lists your opening hours and that a page describes a specific service. Step 4: Strengthen the signals beyond your website

AI assistants do not rely on your website alone. They also look at what other people say about you. Reviews, local news coverage, industry association listings, community sponsorships and mentions on respected websites all help build a picture of who you are and how well you do your work. When those sources are sparse, assistants have less to go on.

Reviews deserve special attention. Encourage happy customers to leave honest reviews on the platforms that matter in your industry, and ask them to mention the specific service they received. A review that says "they replaced our water heater the same afternoon" tells an assistant far more than five stars on their own. Respond to reviews professionally, including the critical ones, because your replies become part of the public record too.

Look for legitimate opportunities to be mentioned elsewhere. A dental clinic might contribute advice to a local parenting blog. A manufacturer might make sure its trade association listing carries an accurate product description. A law firm might publish practical guides that local media reference. Each credible mention gives assistants another trustworthy source that agrees with your website.

Finally, if an assistant states something plainly false, use whatever feedback option it offers. Many assistants let you rate or flag a response. It will not fix the problem on its own, but it costs nothing. The lasting fix is always better information at the source. Step 5: Check again every quarter

AI assistants are updated constantly. Their underlying models change, the sources they search shift and the way they phrase answers evolves. A clean audit today does not guarantee a clean result in six months. Treat this as ongoing maintenance, not a one-time project.

Set a recurring reminder to repeat your audit every three months. Use the same questions and the same spreadsheet so you can compare results over time. Note what improved, what stayed the same and any new mistakes that appeared. Over a year, you will see clearly whether your efforts are paying off.

You should also run a quick check after any significant change. If you move offices, add a new service, change your hours, rebrand or open a second location, update your website and listings first, then check the assistants again a few weeks later. Changes take time to spread, so do not panic if old information lingers for a while. Keep the official sources accurate and the rest usually follows.

Assign this task to a specific person on your team so it does not slip through the cracks. It typically takes an hour or two each quarter. If your team is stretched thin, ongoing website maintenance can include regular content checks, so your site stays accurate without adding to anyone's workload.

Ready to find out what AI is saying about your business?

You cannot control every answer an AI assistant gives, but you can make the accurate answer the easiest one for it to find. Consistent listings, a clear website, genuine reviews and a regular audit routine put you in a far stronger position than competitors who have not looked at this yet.

Most of the work is careful housekeeping, but a few focused hours now can protect your reputation for years and support your traditional search visibility at the same time.
